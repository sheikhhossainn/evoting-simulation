Civic-grade UI · trust over engagement

A calm, legible design system for a cryptographic voting system

Built for three real personas — Voter, Key Holder, and Auditor/Observer — against the flows they actually follow in the codebase. The visual language is deliberately restrained: enough structure that anyone can follow the flow at a glance, without the gloss that would undercut trust. Design references are government digital services (GOV.UK, Vote.gov, Estonia i-Voting) and bank-grade identity & irreversible-action confirmation patterns.

Voter Key Holder Auditor / Observer

Ballot-secrecy rule (non-negotiable). What this screen must NOT do
✗
Never show “You voted for [candidate]”. A confirmation that echoes the choice becomes a coercion receipt — a voter could be forced to show it. It also contradicts the whole encrypted-ballot design.
What it shows instead
  • Verification code — derived from nullifier + ciphertext, not the choice
  • Timestamp and anchoring status
  • A path to verify inclusion later on the public Watchdog

Design system v1

One system, three personas, one promise

Clarity over cleverness. Every screen answers “where am I, what happens next, and what did the system just guarantee me” — without ever revealing how anyone voted.
